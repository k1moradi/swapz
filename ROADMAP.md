# swapz roadmap\n\nThis is the project-level version plan. `VALIDATION.md` records evidence,\n`CODEX_VALIDATION.md` is the active tester contract, and `TODO.md` is the actionable checklist.\n\nFuture version numbers after V2.2 are proposed labels. They may be renumbered before work\nstarts, but the milestone boundaries should remain separate so one major experimental\nvariable changes at a time.\n\n## Project principles\n\n- Primary target: dedicated slow block devices: HDD, USB flash, SD/eMMC, and older SATA SSD.\n- Optimize especially for low queue depth and reclaim behavior, not NVMe throughput.\n- Never depend on compression for capacity and never depend on lower DISCARD for correctness.\n- Preserve old good data until a replacement is safely authoritative.\n- Measure upper completion/reclaim rate, physical drain, swap-in latency, lower bytes/I/Os, CPU, GC, and RAM cost together.\n- Keep streaming-policy, on-disk-format, and allocator experiments in separate milestones.\n- Codex is the secondary tester. Feature/architecture work belongs to the primary developer.\n\n## Frozen history\n\n### V1 — whole-live-set compaction baseline\n\nPreserved: `v1` @ `025edff9fbe1d402cf6eab294e3ce1627e49a1e9`.\n\nEstablished the basic volatile LZ4 Device Mapper design, but allocator rotation copied the\nwhole live set. Partial/incompressible churn caused about 2.86x raw lower writes and about\none-second rotation stalls on the 1 ms delayed stack.\n\n### V2 — segment-local GC\n\nPreserved: `v2` @ `1e3a0c71b4e514bccae58d79be9f319640478b5c`.\n\nIntroduced 1 MiB segments, low-live victim cleaning, bounded reverse scratch, and per-block\nlive accounting. Correctness passed and GC/write amplification improved substantially, but\nsynchronous 4 KiB lower requests remained the throughput bottleneck.\n\n### V2.1 — serialized physical-write batching\n\nPreserved: `v2.1` @ `c841a589a44ada3561a8bbed83a4db86e530ee1c`.\n\nAdded up to 32 KiB serialized lower writes. QD8 partial/incompressible workloads improved\nabout 5.6-6x over V2 with roughly 87% fewer lower write requests. This is the frozen\npre-streaming baseline.\n\n## Current development line\n\n### V2.2 — asynchronous double-buffer streaming\n\nCorrectness baseline: `v2.2-correctness-pass` @ `0f40f52956ff0b4ffc18549b916a54ce55847c02`.\n\nGoal: compare `immediate`, `opportunistic`, and `staged` policies with a configurable\n4 KiB..1 MiB batch ceiling. One lower write may be in flight while the other preallocated\nbuffer fills. Staged mode may early-complete compressed pages from bounded RAM.\n\nStatus: **FULL CORRECTNESS PASS — PERFORMANCE GATE OPEN**.\n\nRemaining V2.2 work:\n\n- find the measured batch-size throughput/read-latency plateau;
- do not rely on null_blk's 20 MiB/s `mbps` throttle above its ~409.6 KiB per-tick request
  budget; use a size-aware backend for 512 KiB/1 MiB if the plateau remains unresolved;\n- compare 0.25/0.5/1/2 ms command latency;\n- compare QD1/8/32/64;\n- compare 100/50/0% compressibility;\n- quantify staged early-completion and cancellation benefit;\n- confirm GC behavior at candidate points;\n- run bounded real swap pressure at candidate points;\n- select the strategy winner and physical-test configuration.\n\nExit criterion: one strategy winner, a measured plateau (or explicit plateau-not-reached\nresult), correctness still green, and a concrete physical-test configuration.\n\nDo not add byte-tight format work or hibernation support during V2.2.\n\n## Proposed future versions\n\n### V2.3 — physical-media qualification and default selection\n\nGoal: validate the V2.2 winner on the real hardware class swapz targets before changing\nthe disk format.\n\nTarget media: HDD, USB flash, SD/eMMC, and older SATA SSD, especially serialized devices\naround 20 MiB/s and more broadly 10-100 MiB/s.\n\nMeasure raw vs swapz throughput, QD1/QD8 first, 100/50/0% compressibility, physical drain,\nlower sectors/I/Os, swap-in latency, CPU, GC cost, staged metrics, RAM cost, DISCARD\nbehavior, and meaningful device health/write counters where available.\n\nPerformance objective: keep incompressible QD1 near raw, keep the physical device near its\nsequential plateau, and make 60-80 MiB/s logical swapout plausible on ~20 MiB/s media when\nmeasured effective physical-byte reduction is 3-4x.\n\nExit criterion: the current-format streaming winner survives real slow media and a default\nstrategy/batch policy is selected from measurements.\n\nNo physical device is touched without explicit authorization of the exact disposable\ndevice or partition.\n\n### V2.4 — byte-tight packed-record experiment\n\nGoal: test physical-density improvement separately from streaming.\n\nKeep 4 KiB logical pages and per-page LZ4/random decode. Compare a byte-tight packed format\nagainst the V2.3 winner on lower bytes, I/Os, throughput, swap-in latency, CPU, metadata RAM,\nand GC. Do not combine this with a new allocator design.\n\nExit criterion: keep the byte-tight format only if its physical-media benefit is material\nand it does not cause unacceptable read-latency, metadata, or GC regression. Otherwise keep\nthe simpler 4 KiB-container format.\n\n### V2.5 — deployment and boot integration\n\nGoal: turn the validated data path into a clean deployable Linux component.\n\nPlanned work: DKMS/package install/uninstall, stable `swapzctl` configuration, systemd\nactivation after real root as the default, stable `/dev/disk/by-*` backing identifiers,\nsafe start/stop/restart, upgrade behavior, identity diagnostics, and recovery docs.\n\nOptional Dracut early-swap integration may be added for very low-memory systems, but it is\nopt-in and is not hibernation support.\n\nExit criterion: normal boot/shutdown deployment is repeatable and safe, and optional early\nswap does not become a default boot-critical dependency.\n\n### V2.6 — hardening / volatile release candidate\n\nGoal: freeze the non-hibernation architecture before persistence work.\n\nPlanned work: long-duration churn and pressure, repeated GC and fault injection, lifecycle\nstress, negative configuration tests, packaging/upgrade regressions, performance regression\nthresholds, documentation freeze, and a stable volatile-swap release candidate.\n\nKeep current constraints unless separately justified: 4 KiB PAGE_SIZE, dedicated block\nbacking, LZ4, no capacity overcommit, no swapfile/filesystem backing, and no encryption in\nswapz itself.\n\nExit criterion: no known correctness blocker, measured performance/deployment envelope\ndocumented, and a stable volatile release candidate exists.\n\n## V3.0 — final persistent hibernate/resume version\n\nHibernate/resume is deferred during V2.x, but it is a required final-version milestone.\n\nThis must be a separate major version because the current mapping/staged state is volatile.\nResume after power loss/reboot requires persistent state, boot-time discovery, compatibility\nrules, and explicit failure recovery.\n\nRequired design:\n\n- versioned persistent on-disk metadata/data format;\n- power-loss-safe publication/commit protocol;\n- strict separation of disposable swap state from resumable hibernation state;\n- corruption/integrity detection and safe rejection;\n- format compatibility/version handling;\n- backing-device identity checks;\n- early boot discovery and activation;\n- Dracut/initramfs integration;\n- correct kernel resume handoff;\n- cleanup/invalidation after successful or abandoned resume.\n\nRequired validation:\n\n- hibernate -> poweroff -> boot -> resume;\n- repeated cycles;\n- interrupted/incomplete hibernation;\n- corrupt/partial metadata;\n- unsupported format version;\n- missing/replaced backing device;\n- stale state on cold boot;\n- failed-resume fallback;\n- regression of all ordinary swap correctness/performance tests.\n\nV3.0 exit criterion: ordinary volatile swap remains correct and fast, hibernation data is\npersistent and safely discoverable, resume works across reboot/poweroff, and corrupt/stale\nstate fails safely.\n\n## Version discipline\n\nBefore starting a future version:\n\n1. freeze the previous validated baseline;\n2. state one primary hypothesis for the new version;\n3. define correctness gates before performance work;\n4. retain the previous version as the matched control;\n5. keep test/docs commits separate from kernel changes where practical;\n6. record exact tested commit and loaded-module identity;\n7. never declare a performance win from upper completion rate alone;\n8. do not start the next architectural experiment while a correctness failure is unresolved.
